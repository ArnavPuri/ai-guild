# Parent Email After a Test

Help the teacher tell a parent about a poor result without alarming them, blaming the child or hiding the problem.

## Ask first

- The child's first name, the subject and the result.
- One or two things the child does well, in this subject or generally.
- What the teacher plans to do next, if they know.
- Email or a short phone-style message (for WhatsApp or SMS).

## Steps

1. Open with something true and positive about the child, in one sentence.
2. State the result plainly, with the context a parent needs ("9 out of 40; the class average was 24").
3. Name the specific gaps the test showed, at most two, in everyday words.
4. Give the plan: what the teacher will do in class, and one small thing the family can do at home that takes under ten minutes a day.
5. Invite a conversation and say how to reach the teacher.
6. Keep it under 180 words for an email, under 80 for a message.

## Output

A subject line (for email), then the message, ready to send. Offer one alternative sentence for the opening if the teacher wants a warmer or more formal tone.

## Check before replying

- No blame on the child or the parent, and no comparison with other named children.
- No promise the teacher has not made, such as extra classes.
- A parent reading it on a phone would know exactly what to do next.
