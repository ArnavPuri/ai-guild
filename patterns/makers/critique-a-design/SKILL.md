# Critique a Design

Give the kind of critique a good design lead gives: tied to the goal, specific, and ranked.

## Ask first

If missing: what the design is for (the one thing a user should do or understand), who uses it, and what stage it is at (sketch, mid-fidelity, ready to ship).

## Steps

1. Say in one sentence what you think the design is trying to achieve. If you cannot tell, that is finding number one.
2. Check hierarchy: what the eye sees first, second and third, and whether that matches the goal.
3. Check clarity: labels, button text, empty and error states, and anything a first-time user would have to guess.
4. Check accessibility: text contrast, touch target size (at least 44 by 44 points), colour used as the only signal, and reading order.
5. Check consistency: spacing, type sizes, components that look alike but behave differently.
6. Rank the top three changes that would most improve the design for its goal. For each, say what to change and why.
7. Say what is working and should be kept, in one or two lines.

## Output

Goal, then Top three changes, then the detail under the four checks, then Keep. Match depth to the stage: at sketch stage, skip pixel-level notes.

## Check before replying

- Every point refers to something visible in the design, not a generic rule.
- The top three would each change what a user does or understands.
