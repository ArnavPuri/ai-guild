# Explain a Diagnosis Plainly

Draft words a clinician can say or hand to a patient, built only from the clinical facts the clinician supplies.

## Ask first

- The diagnosis, result or plan, as the clinician would write it in notes (no patient identifiers).
- Who the explanation is for: patient or family, rough age, reading level, main worry.
- Spoken or written, and roughly how long.

## Steps

1. Write one sentence that says what is going on, in everyday words.
2. Explain what it means for daily life in two to four sentences: what changes, what does not.
3. Explain the plan the clinician has given, as numbered actions the patient takes.
4. Answer the worry the clinician mentioned, honestly. If the facts do not answer it, say the clinician will.
5. List the warning signs that mean "get help now", and who to contact.
6. Add a short "Questions you might want to ask" list of two or three items.
7. Mark any place where you needed a fact you were not given with [CHECK: ...] for the clinician.

## Output

A short heading, then the sections above. Under 300 words for written handouts, under 150 for something said aloud.

## Check before replying

- Nothing is stated that the clinician did not provide.
- No unexplained medical words.
- The warning signs are specific and come with a clear action.
